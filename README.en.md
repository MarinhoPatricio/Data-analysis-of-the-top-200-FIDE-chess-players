# Data Analysis of the FIDE top 200

<p> The data shown here was acquired via web scraping from the FIDE (International Chess Federation) website. The discriminatory factor used to define them as “greatest chess players” was their current standard rating. Thus, I removed deceased players from the analysis and disregarded the historical peak of living players. The list thus displays the names of 200 players.

### By federation / country

<p> Note: the country to which they will be linked here is not necessarily their country of birth, but rather the one for which they play.


<p> Dividing them by country, India and the United States share the first place with 17 grandmasters each. They are followed by China and Armenia, with 13 and 10 players, respectively. Of the ten countries with the most players on the list, 5 are Asian (considering Russia as an Asian country), 3 are European, 1 is American and the rest is... well... FIDE.

<p> Choose to compete under the FIDE flag players who — among other reasons — want independence, who are in the process of changing nationalities or who, more recently, are Russian players. Because of the Russian-Ukrainian war, FIDE has banned the usage of Russian and Belarusian national symbols. As a result, players from these countries, in official matches, play using the flag of the international federation. If you look at the list, you’ll see that all 9 of the players who appear in the FIDE federation are actually Russian.

### By generation

<p> When players are segmented by generation, the graph shows the strength of the millennial generation: It makes up 51.5% of all players analyzed, with 103 GMs. As they are from the generation born between 1981 and 1996, they will still be high performance players for some time; especially those born closer to the end of the generation. It is also worth saying that this is the generation of Magnus Carlsen, five-time world champion and holder of the highest rating in history

<p align="center">
    <img src="https://i.imgur.com/qTr3FBg.png" width="50%"/>
</p>


<p>	In second place is the zoomer generation, with 35.5%. With 71 players, those born between 1997 and 2012 have a fairly solid representation in the sport. The trend is for them to overtake the millenials in a few years.

<p>	A geração X, por sua vez, totaliza 12,5% com seus 25 jogadores. Os jogadores que nasceram entre 1965 e 1980, embora tenham uma vasta experiência, já passaram dos 35 anos, marco tido como o início do declínio da performance profissional dos enxadristas. 

<p> The boomer generation, on the other hand, has only one player on the list. Among them, born between 1946 and 1964, only the Russian Vladimir Afromeev is listed. Curiously, he's the only one on the list who doesn't hold the title of grandmaster. He is a FIDE Master, which makes some people question the fairness of his results. 

<p> There is also a different generational profile between the countries: India has a more zoomer profile; the United States, a more balanced one between zoomers and millennials; China, on its turn, is mostly millennial, and Russia (including the Russian FIDE players) is mainly zoomer, followed by millennials and a significant presence of generation X — in addition to the only boomer player on the list, as mentioned before.

### Top 10


<p> Analyzing the top ten players with the highest rating, we see India and the United States at the top again, with 3 and 2 players, respectively. They are followed by China, with 1 player. The remaining countries (Russia, France, Uzbekistan and Norway) also have 1 player each. The generational profile is young, with 4 millennials, 5 zoomers and 1 from generation X.

<p> There are two former world champions in this chart: Magnus and Anand. The first, by the way, was responsible for taking the title away from the player listed in tenth place. That was in the 2013 world championship. Since then Magnus has won four more championships, but for his own reasons he didn't compete for the 2023 championship, which was then played by the Russian Ian Nepomniachtchi - who appears in eighth place in the chart - and the Chinese Ding Liren - who doesn't even appear in the top 10. With the Chinese victory in 2023, Ding will defend his title in this year's championship against India's Gukesh, who appears in fifth place. The irony, as the legendary grandmaster and former champion Kasparov pointed out, is that this will be a championship without the best player in the world (Magnus).

> "My hottest take is that I don't treat it as a world championship match. For me a world championship match was always the match for the title of the best player in the world. I think the history of the world championship matches, it started, by the way, here in St. Louis, with Steinitz facing Zukertort back in 1886, has ended with Magnus Carlsen. There were 16 world champions, you could call them at every given moment the best players.”
